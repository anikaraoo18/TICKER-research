nterpretation A: Capital-Preservation & Liability-First Focus
This interpretation treats the mandate through a risk-mitigation lens, prioritizing absolute capital protection, strict enforcement of the loss ceiling, and guaranteed safety for near-term distributions.
1. Objective
Primary Goal: Maximize the probability of fully funding all scheduled quarterly distributions ($750,000) with zero capital disruption, while strictly keeping calendar-year realized losses below the 10% ceiling under all tested conditions.
2. Hard Constraints
Liquidity Buffer: Exactly or greater than $3,000,000 (12 months of distributions) held continuously in cash or daily-liquid U.S. government securities.
Drawdown Circuit Breaker: If trailing year-to-date losses reach 8%, the portfolio must automatically freeze further risk-seeking asset purchases and transition available cash flows into short-term government paper to prevent hitting the 10% hard stop.
Portfolio Limits: Long-only, no derivatives, short sales, or leverage. Single-issuer cap at 8%. Annual turnover capped at 25%. At least 90% of total assets redeemable within five business days.
3. Invented Assumptions
Definition of Loss Limit: The 10% calendar-year loss constraint is interpreted as an absolute realized-loss ceiling. A breach is treated as a severe policy violation requiring immediate asset reallocation to cash.
Pilot Optimization Metric: The pilot will optimize for minimum downside semi-variance and maximum distribution coverage ratio, treating the 60/40 benchmark as a secondary reference rather than an active tracking target.
4. Validation Tests
Historical Stress-Testing: Subject the proposed portfolio framework to historical crisis periods (such as 2008 and 2022) to verify that calendar-year drawdowns never exceed 10% and that liquidity buffers remain entirely uncompromised.
Liquidity Stress Test: Simulate a rapid 5-day redemption cycle across 90% of the portfolio under stressed market depth conditions to confirm compliance with the 5-day rule.
5. Crucial CIO Question
"Does the 10 percent calendar-year loss limit represent an absolute realized-loss ceiling that triggers mandatory de-risking when approached, or is it a statistical Value-at-Risk (VaR) confidence limit over a one-year horizon?"

Interpretation B: Return-Target & Benchmark-Tracking Focus
This interpretation treats the mandate through an asset-growth lens, focusing squarely on achieving the CPI + 4% rolling five-year return objective and maintaining a disciplined relationship with the 60/40 policy benchmark.
1. Objective
Primary Goal: Maximize expected annualized return over rolling five-year periods to achieve or exceed CPI + 4% net of costs, while maintaining a controlled tracking error relative to the 60% global equities / 40% U.S. investment-grade bonds benchmark.
2. Hard Constraints
Benchmark Alignment: Portfolio construction must stay within defined tactical deviation bands around the 60% equity / 40% bond policy mix.
Risk-Budget Management: Annual turnover must not exceed 25% without committee approval; single-issuer exposure capped at 8%.
Liquidity & Distributions: Maintain the $3,000,000 cash/government security buffer for distributions, ensuring 90% of total assets remain redeemable within five business days under ordinary conditions. No borrowing, shorting, or derivatives.
3. Invented Assumptions
Definition of Loss Limit: The 10% calendar-year loss constraint is interpreted as a statistical probability threshold (e.g., 95% confidence interval) rather than a rigid zero-tolerance circuit breaker, acknowledging that rare market tails may occasionally breach it without invalidating the long-term strategy.
Pilot Optimization Metric: The pilot will optimize for Sharpe ratio and tracking error minimization against the 60/40 policy benchmark, evaluating success based on excess return generation relative to risk.
4. Validation Tests
Monte Carlo Simulation: Run multi-thousand path simulations over 7-year horizons to calculate the statistical probability of meeting the CPI + 4% net return target across various inflation regimes.
Tracking Error Attribution: Analyze historical factor exposures to ensure the portfolio's active risk relative to the 60/40 benchmark stays within a tight, predictable band.
5. Crucial CIO Question
"Should the pilot optimize primarily for tracking error and risk-adjusted return relative to the 60/40 policy benchmark, or should it treat the CPI + 4% hurdle as an absolute return target that permits tactical departures from the benchmark when equities underperform inflation?"
